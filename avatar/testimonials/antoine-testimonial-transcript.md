## Avatar Notes

**Before-state language, unprompted.** *Monotone* is his word, used first and repeatedly.
The rest is social consequence, not vocal technique: "people were thinking I was shouting at
them," "they were a bit scared of me," "pushing people away." He did not arrive wanting to
sing — he arrived having already noticed that his voice was costing him the room. That is
the entrepreneur avatar's actual entry wound, and he states it without prompting.

**The objection he names.** Vulnerability, twice, in both recordings: "I was really
uncomfortable singing. Something I've never really done in public" (ref 1 · 01:15) and "I was
a bit scared at first going in, putting myself vulnerable" (ref 3 · 00:56). He then supplies
the reframe himself — "because it made me uncomfortable, I needed to do it" — which lands
harder from a buyer than from a seller. Pair the objection and the reframe in the same cut;
splitting them wastes the strongest thirty seconds he gives.

**The transformation he leads with.** Business outcome, not artistry. He runs public speaking
workshops and pitch practice for entrepreneurs, and reports carrying the method into them
directly. The mechanism he articulates is the VME argument in one line: breath control in
singing is "three levels above normal speaking," so public speaking becomes "really easy for
you." Over-training. It is the clearest causal claim in either testimonial and it is still
uncut.

**Why he is the VME proof and Irina is not.** He is the only student who did both programs,
he is a non-native speaker, and he converts the singing work into revenue-adjacent skill.
Every claim VME makes about entrepreneurs is evidenced somewhere in these three files, in a
customer's voice.

**Second-language angle.** ref 3 · 03:48 is a complete segment for non-native-speaker
prospects — and note the direction he takes it: not accent elimination but "being comfortable
with sharing your personal touch through your unique accent." That is on-mission. Do not cut
it into an accent-correction promise; it is the opposite claim.

**Name check.** He introduces himself as "Anthony" on camera. Confirm which name he wants on
lower-thirds and captions before publishing.

**Machine-transcription caveat.** ref 2 · 04:16 has an unrecoverable gap — "I would attribute
this 90% to [unclear]." Do not cut that line without relistening; the missing words carry the
attribution.
