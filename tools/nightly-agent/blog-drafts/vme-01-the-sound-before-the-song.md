# The Sound Before the Song

Most people think a lesson starts when the music starts. It doesn't. It starts with a sound that isn't music at all — a low, unglamorous buzz, lips loose, nothing pretty about it. Varun makes that sound in the first ten seconds of this clip, and if you didn't know better you'd think the lesson hadn't begun yet.

It had. That buzz is doing more work than the chorus that comes an hour later.

I have watched hundreds of students walk in wanting to sing the big note first. The buzz is the opposite instinct — it strips the voice down to nothing but breath and vibration, no vowel to hide behind, no lyric to distract you from what your air is actually doing. You can't fake your way through it. Either the breath is supporting the sound or the sound collapses, right there, in front of everyone.

Watch Varun's face in the clip. That's not effort. That's a person listening to his own instrument for the first time in a way he probably hasn't since he was a kid making noise for no reason. That's the whole point of a warmup — not to prepare the voice for the song, but to remind the person that the voice was never separate from them to begin with.
