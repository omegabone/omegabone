# The Part Nobody Claps For

Ira's "Arranging the Intros and Outros" is a small, unglamorous kind of clip — nobody's belting, nobody's crying, nobody's landing a big note. It's the part of a performance most audiences never consciously register: how a song actually begins and ends when a real person is singing it live, not just how it sounds on the record.

That's exactly why it's worth watching. The way you walk into a phrase and the way you leave it is doing more work on your speaking voice than people give it credit for — it's the difference between a sentence that trails off and one that lands, between an opening line that just starts and one that pulls a room in before you've said anything memorable yet. Singers rehearse this on purpose. Most speakers never do, and then wonder why a good point gets lost in a mumbled first ten seconds or an ending nobody realizes was the ending.

Arranging an intro or an outro is a small act of respect for whoever's listening — you're deciding, on purpose, how you want to arrive and how you want to leave. Keep an eye out for more of this kind of detail work once Frequency is further along; it's exactly the sort of thing that's easy to skip past in a highlight reel and impossible to skip past once you're the one on stage.
