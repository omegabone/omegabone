# A Sound With No Song In It Yet

There's a version of this clip where Varun skips straight to a lyric and it would look like a completely different lesson. Instead he sits in a buzz — lips together, air moving, nothing recognizable as singing — and stays there far longer than most people would have the patience for.

That patience is the lesson. A buzz has nowhere to hide. There's no melody to carry it, no words to distract the ear, just breath meeting the lips in a straight line. If the airflow is uneven, the buzz sputters. If the breath is being held instead of released, the buzz goes thin and disappears. You hear the truth of what the breath is doing before a single vowel ever gets involved.

What I notice watching Varun here is how much he's listening rather than performing. Most people, asked to make a strange sound in front of someone, perform it — they add flair, they apologize with their face, they rush through it to get back to something that feels more like "real singing." Varun doesn't rush it. He treats the buzz like information, not like an embarrassment to get past.

That's the actual use of an exercise like this. It isn't a warmup in the sense of loosening something up before the real work starts. It is the real work, just without an audience yet.
