# The Breath Before the Voice

Adri hasn't sung a single word yet in this clip and it's already the most important thirty seconds of the lesson.

I say this to every new student and most of them don't believe me until they see it happen to someone else first: the voice doesn't start at the vocal folds. It starts lower, earlier, quieter — in the breath you took before you ever opened your mouth. Watch Adri's shoulders. Watch how still they stay while everything underneath them is doing the actual work. That stillness up top is not an accident. It's the whole exercise.

What I love about this particular moment is that it isn't polished. It's a person mid-discovery, working out in real time what "breathing for singing" actually feels like versus what breathing has felt like for the previous however-many years of just staying alive. Those are two different skills wearing the same name, and almost nobody teaches the second one directly. We tend to jump straight to the song and hope the breath sorts itself out.

It doesn't, usually. It has to be built, on purpose, the way Adri is building it here.

This is exactly the kind of foundational, unglamorous groundwork we're building a full program around under the Frequency name — so if instrument-level work like this is interesting to you, keep an eye out for it.
