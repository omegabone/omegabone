# The Breath You Forgot You Had

Everyone already knows how to breathe. That's the problem.

You've been doing it, unconsciously, correctly enough to stay alive, for your entire life — and that unconscious competence is exactly what makes it so hard to unlearn the shallow, chest-high, adrenaline version of it that shows up the second you're asked to sing in front of someone. Watch Varun in this clip working his way back down to the breath underneath that reflexive one. It's not a new skill. It's an old one, buried.

What strikes me every time I watch a student go through this is how much it looks like remembering rather than learning. Nobody teaches a baby to breathe low and wide — babies just do it, bellies rising and falling with nothing held, nothing braced. Somewhere between then and now, most of us trade that breath in for a smaller, faster, more anxious one and never notice the swap. A good warmup doesn't install something foreign. It goes looking for the breath you already had.

You can see that search happening here, in real time, with nothing else going on — no song, no lyric, just Varun and his own air, finding its way back to where it used to live.
