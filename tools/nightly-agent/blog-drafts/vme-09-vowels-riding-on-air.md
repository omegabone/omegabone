# Vowels Riding on Air

Watch Varun's mouth in this clip and you'll notice it isn't doing very much. That's the point. The shape of a vowel is a small, almost lazy adjustment. What actually carries the sound across a room is everything happening underneath it, out of frame, in the breath.

Most singers get this backwards early on. They treat the vowel as the source of the sound and the breath as an afterthought — something you top up between phrases so you don't run out. Here it's the opposite order. The breath is the engine. The vowel just rides on top of it, shaped but not straining, because straining a vowel to be louder or bigger is really just breath being asked to do a job it was never built to do that way.

There's a moment in this clip where you can see Varun stop pushing the sound and let the air carry it instead, and the tone opens up without him doing anything that looks like "more effort." That's not a coincidence and it's not luck. It's what happens when the breath is actually connected to the vowel instead of the vowel trying to survive on its own.

This is the kind of connection — breath to shape, shape to sound — that a lot of the work under the Frequency name is going to be built around. Keep an eye out for it.
